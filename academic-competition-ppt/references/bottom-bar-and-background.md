# Bottom Bar And Background

Default long-form paper decks need visible academic chrome, but the default refined deep-teal style should stay restrained and readable. Do not leave ordinary content pages as pure white canvases, and do not turn the footer into a heavy second navigation system.

## Bottom Takeaway Bar

- Ordinary content pages should include a bottom takeaway bar.
- Cover and chapter divider pages may use special treatment.
- Height: about 3%-7% of slide height; keep it thin-to-medium unless the slide needs stronger academic closure.
- Colors: pale yellow, pale green, warm ivory, sage green, deep teal, or low-opacity theme band, with deep-teal/yellow accents used sparingly.
- Content: one-sentence conclusion, research boundary, or transition prompt.
- The takeaway must be specific to the page, not a generic slogan.
- The main purpose of the bottom bar is visual closure and polish. It should feel like part of the template, not a random note pasted onto the slide.
- Decorative elements such as arcs, leaves, arrows, soft geometric pieces, or corner motifs are allowed, but they must be lightweight, low-opacity, and low-area. They should not occupy a large part of the bottom bar or compete with the takeaway text.
- Prefer thin or medium bars, soft gradients, translucent overlays, subtle shadows, and gentle highlights for bottom-bar blocks and decorative fills. Default gradients may blend low-saturation deep teal, pale teal, and pale yellow. Avoid flat heavy blocks.
- Corner chrome is allowed: quiet page markers, small deep-teal corner bands, low-opacity geometric blocks, or paired footer/header accents.
- Keep decorative elements behind or beside the takeaway. They must not reduce text contrast, obscure page numbers, or pull attention away from the content.

Examples:

- `本页要点：合规成本既可能挤出资源，也可能形成绿色资本。`
- `过渡：理解变量定义后，下一步是解释这些变量如何进入模型。`
- `研究边界：待估计结果不能写成已完成实证结论。`

## Background Decoration

- Avoid large pure-white empty backgrounds.
- Use high-transparency decoration, about 85%-95% transparency.
- Allowed: pale green geometric blocks, translucent arcs, low-opacity grid, corner abstract blocks, subtle bottom bands, deep-teal title bars, and light card-edge layers.
- Decoration must not interfere with evidence readability.
- Decoration can be visually expressive, but it should stay secondary: small-to-medium area, soft edge, controlled contrast, and never over the main reading path.
- Body card backgrounds and transparent rounded rectangles may use soft gradient fills instead of single flat borders. Use low-contrast gradient edges, translucent stacked layers, or light shadows to create depth without reducing readability.
- Chapter pages may use deep green banners or large color blocks for rhythm. Ordinary content pages should stay mostly white/pale-gray, but may use a deep-teal title bar, visible bottom/corner chrome, or slightly stronger background decoration when it improves structure.

## Visual Baseline

Use the current refined deep-teal navigation version as the default baseline for:

- white or pale gray body pages;
- deep teal academic identity;
- deep-teal top navigation with yellow active chapter chip and lighter inactive chips;
- subtle or moderately visible background texture;
- bottom takeaway bar, gradient footer, or quiet corner chrome;
- dense but readable cards, formulas, and evidence panels.

Do not use the sparse later version as the visual default.
