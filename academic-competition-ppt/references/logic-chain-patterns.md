# Logic Chain Patterns

Use this for long-form explanation pacing. The deck should feel like a sequence of questions being answered, not a compressed table of contents.

## Core Rhythm

`follow-up question -> paper answer -> proof object -> plain-language translation -> next follow-up question`.

Each major chapter should contain at least one question transition page.

## Common Follow-Up Questions

- Why is this problem important?
- Why might the common intuition be wrong?
- Why is the existing literature not enough?
- Why does the concept need this measurement?
- Why would a naive method be biased?
- Why is the main effect not enough?
- Why do we need mechanism, heterogeneity, or robustness?
- Why does this model fit the economic story?
- What does the coefficient mean in behavior or policy terms?
- What can this result not prove?

## Long-Form Explanation Grammar

Borrow the teaching grammar of long paper seminars such as "第18期绿色倾斜" without copying its content or visuals:

- Open with a real-world paradox rather than an abstract definition.
- State the wrong or incomplete intuition before introducing the paper's method.
- Use examples to explain technical concepts.
- Break formulas into economic behavior, variables, decisions, constraints, and interpretation.
- Translate results into memorable claims tied back to the research question.
- Use recap pages so the audience remembers what has been proven.

## Transition Page Contract

A question transition page should include:

- What has just been established.
- The new doubt or follow-up question.
- Why the next section is necessary.
- A small logic-chain map or breadcrumb when the deck is long.
